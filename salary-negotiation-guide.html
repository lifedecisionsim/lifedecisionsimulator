How to Negotiate Your Salary (Step-by-Step Guide)

Why Salary Negotiation Matters

Negotiating your salary is one of the highest-return skills in your career. A small increase today compounds over years.

For example, a $5,000 raise doesn't just mean $5,000 more this year — it affects your entire future earning trajectory.

Not sure how that compounds? See our full 5-Year Salary Growth Guide to calculate the long-term impact.

Step 1: Know Your Market Value

Before negotiating, research:

  • Industry average salary for your role
  • Location-based compensation differences
  • Your experience level

Confidence comes from data.

Step 2: Choose the Right Timing

The best time to negotiate:

  • After strong performance reviews
  • After completing a major project
  • During promotion discussions

Avoid negotiating during company downturns or budget freezes.

Step 3: Build Your Case

Prepare measurable proof:

  • Revenue generated
  • Cost savings created
  • Performance improvements
  • New responsibilities taken on

Employers respond to impact, not emotion.

Step 4: Use a Clear Negotiation Script

Example script:

"Based on my performance over the past year and current market benchmarks, I believe an adjustment to $X would better reflect my contributions and responsibilities."

Stay calm, confident, and professional.

Step 5: Calculate the Long-Term Impact

Before accepting or rejecting an offer, project the long-term difference.

For example:

  • Current salary: $60,000
  • Negotiated salary: $65,000
  • Over 5 years at 4% growth — that difference becomes significant.

What If They Say No?

If your request is denied:

  • Ask what goals you must hit to qualify for an increase
  • Request a timeline for reconsideration
  • Evaluate external opportunities

Sometimes switching jobs accelerates salary growth — read more in our Salary Growth Projection Guide.

Common Salary Negotiation Mistakes

  • Not doing market research
  • Negotiating emotionally
  • Accepting the first offer immediately
  • Failing to calculate long-term impact

Key Takeaways

  • Negotiation compounds over time.
  • Preparation and timing are crucial.
  • Always calculate 5-year impact before deciding.
