How to Use a Decision Matrix for Life Choices

When facing big decisions like changing careers, accepting a job offer, or starting a business, emotions can cloud judgment. A decision matrix helps evaluate options logically by assigning values to important factors.

If your matrix shows low satisfaction at work, try our quit job readiness simulator to see if it’s time to move on.

Step 1: Define Your Options

  • Stay at current job
  • Accept a new job offer
  • Start a business

Step 2: Assign Values

Example scoring (1–10 scale):

  • Stay at current job → Salary: 6 | Stress: 7 | Growth: 5
  • Accept job offer → Salary: 8 | Stress: 6 | Growth: 8
  • Start business → Salary: 5 | Stress: 5 | Growth: 10

Step 3: Set Importance (Weights)

Salary: 40% | Stress: 20% | Growth: 40%

Step 4: Calculate Scores

Each option is scored by combining values and weights. Higher scores indicate better overall outcomes.

Step 5: Simulate Outcomes

Simulation introduces randomness to reflect uncertainty in real life decisions, giving a more realistic result.

Step 6: Results Visualization

Conclusion

A decision matrix helps remove bias and compare options logically. Combined with simulation, it can reveal which choice offers the best expected outcome.
