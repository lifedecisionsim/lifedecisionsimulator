Plan Your Salary Growth Over 5 Years (With Real Examples & Free Calculator)

Why Salary Growth Planning Matters

Understanding your salary growth over the next 5 years is crucial for making informed career and financial decisions. Planning ahead helps you:

  • Set realistic income goals
  • Decide whether to stay at your current job or seek a promotion

    • Want to increase your projection? Read our Step-by-Step Salary Negotiation Guide to improve your earning potential.

  • Prepare for big financial decisions like buying a home or investing

Without a clear projection, it’s easy to underestimate or overestimate your earning potential.

The Simple Salary Growth Formula

To project your salary over 5 years, you can use this formula:

Future Salary = Current Salary × (1 + Annual Raise %) ^ Number of Years

Where:

  • Current Salary = Your base salary today
  • Annual Raise % = Expected raise each year (as a decimal, e.g., 0.05 for 5%)
  • Number of Years = How far into the future you want to project (5 years in our example)

This formula gives a realistic estimate of your earnings growth over time.

Real 5-Year Salary Scenarios

Example 1: Standard 3% Annual Raise

Suppose your current salary is $50,000. With a 3% raise each year, your 5-year projection is:

  • Year 1: $50,000 × 1.03 = $51,500
  • Year 2: $51,500 × 1.03 ≈ $53,045
  • Year 3: $53,045 × 1.03 ≈ $54,636
  • Year 4: $54,636 × 1.03 ≈ $56,275
  • Year 5: $56,275 × 1.03 ≈ $57,963

Example 2: Aggressive 5% Annual Raise

Same base salary ($50,000), but a 5% raise each year:

  • Year 1: $50,000 × 1.05 = $52,500
  • Year 2: $52,500 × 1.05 ≈ $55,125
  • Year 3: $55,125 × 1.05 ≈ $57,881
  • Year 4: $57,881 × 1.05 ≈ $60,775
  • Year 5: $60,775 × 1.05 ≈ $63,813

Example 3: Job Switch / Big Promotion

Sometimes, changing jobs can lead to a one-time boost plus raises:

  • Year 1: $50,000 → promotion to $60,000
  • Year 2: $60,000 × 1.05 ≈ $63,000
  • Year 3: $63,000 × 1.05 ≈ $66,150
  • Year 4: $66,150 × 1.05 ≈ $69,457
  • Year 5: $69,457 × 1.05 ≈ $72,930

How Inflation & Taxes Affect Salary Growth

Nominal growth is only part of the picture. Inflation and taxes reduce your effective earnings. For example:

  • 5% nominal raise, 2% inflation → 3% real growth
  • Higher tax bracket → net salary grows slower than gross

FAQs

Is a 5% annual raise good?

Yes, a 5% raise is above average in many industries and can significantly grow your salary over 5 years.

How do I project salary in Excel?

Use the formula =CurrentSalary*(1+Raise%)^Years in Excel for each year.

How long before salary growth compounds significantly?

Usually after 3–4 years, even small annual raises can add up noticeably due to compounding.
